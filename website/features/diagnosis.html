Auto Diagnosis & vehicle data

Known answers first. Then the test plan.

Pick the vehicle and describe the concern. You see what’s already known before anyone picks up a wrench: recalls that may cover it, what other owners of the same model report to NHTSA, what fixed it at your shop before, and what the codes mean. An optional AI test plan is built from the same data.

What Auto Diagnosis pulls for this

  • Recalls for the model year
  • Owner complaints for one model year either side, ranked, with common phrases and typical failure mileage
  • Your shop’s matching ROs
  • Code meanings with causes ordered most likely first
  • General first checks, labelled “confirm with the OEM procedure”

Pick the vehicle from the shop, a customer or plate, a scanned or typed VIN, or year/make/model. P, B, C and U codes are picked out of what you type, and opening it from an RO brings in the concern and codes.

Sources

Where the answers come from

Every lead says where it came from, so the tech knows how much weight to give it.

Recalls

NHTSA recalls for the model year. One that may relate to the concern goes to the top of the quick answer.

Owner complaints

What owners reported to NHTSA for one model year either side, ranked against the concern, with common phrases and typical failure mileage.

Fixed here before

Your own ROs on the same make and model, then the same make, with a matching concern, code or cause.

Code meanings

Generic SAE meanings, with causes ordered most likely first. Manufacturer-specific codes are flagged to look up in OEM information.

First checks

General first checks for each concern, labelled “confirm with the OEM procedure.”

AI test plan Optional

Claude builds a test plan from the same data. When you open Auto Diagnosis from an RO, you can save the plan to that RO’s notes. Needs Shop Cloud and your Anthropic key.

Nothing is invented

Vehicle-specific facts come only from NHTSA data or your own repair orders. These are leads to verify, not a diagnosis, and the AI test plan always needs staff review.

  • Recalls and owner reports: NHTSA data
  • Fixed here before: your own repair orders
  • Opened from an RO, the findings go into its notes for the tech to verify

VIN decoder

A VIN decoder that runs on the device

Type or scan a VIN and it decodes from NHTSA data that ships with the app. There’s no outside VIN service.

  • Check digit (ISO 3779), model year and country always work offline, and so does the maker for common manufacturers.
  • The detailed decode for a maker, including six-character WMIs for small manufacturers, downloads once from the app and is kept for offline use.
  • Scan VIN barcodes with the camera.

From NHTSA’s vPIC database (public domain): passenger cars, SUVs, trucks and incomplete chassis. Vehicle data is saved on the device as you use it.

Model-year pages

Engines, drive types, bodies, transmissions, trims and plants, each tied to the VIN codes that select it (including the 8th-digit engine code), plus a VIN pattern table.

Generated system diagrams

Engine layout with cylinder numbering and firing order, engine-control inputs and outputs, starting & charging, drivetrain, cooling or EV thermal loops, brake hydraulics, network topology, OBD-II pins in use, and the high-voltage system on hybrids and EVs.

Parts lists and repair guides

A service parts list per configuration, matched to your inventory, and up to 17 general repair guides chosen for the configuration, with tools, steps and cautions.

Search from the page or the command palette: ⌘K, or Ctrl K on Windows.

Reference shelf

The service library

Factory portals, free documents, wiring references, trouble codes and shop calculators, under Technical in the app or from the command palette.

Service library in the sample shop

OEM service information

22 OEM service-information portals for factory manuals, wiring diagrams, TSBs and programming. They’re links; the subscriptions are yours.

Free documents

33 free official documents, such as body-builder guides and emergency-response guides, plus 15 industry & safety resources.

Wiring & pinouts

An interactive SAE J1962 OBD-II pinout with CAN tests, a relay schematic, fuse colors, OEM wire-color abbreviations, trailer connectors and an AWG table.

Codes & calculators

725 generic OBD-II codes, with causes and checks for the 65 most common. Calculators for voltage drop and wire sizing, Ohm’s law, tire size and unit conversions.

Good to know

  • Needs: internet for NHTSA lookups. They’re free and need no key.
  • Recalls match by year, make and model. They aren’t a VIN-specific open-recall status; there’s a link to NHTSA’s VIN lookup for that.
  • First checks are general, not make-specific.
  • The AI test plan uses Shop Cloud and your Anthropic API key.
  • Diagrams, parts lists and guides are generated, with inferred items labelled typical. Torque values, capacities, component pinouts and wire colors are left to factory service information.
  • It isn’t a replacement for ALLDATA, Mitchell or OEM information.

Questions

Diagnosis questions

Is it a diagnosis?

No. It gives ranked leads to verify, each with its source. The tech still tests the car.

Does it need AI?

No. Recalls, owner reports, your shop’s history, code meanings and first checks all work without it. The AI test plan is optional.

Does the VIN decoder need internet?

It doesn’t use an outside service. Check digit, year and country work offline, and so does the maker for common manufacturers. Once a maker’s detailed data is on the device, that works offline too.

Does it include factory wiring diagrams?

No. The service library links to the OEM service-information portals, and the subscriptions are yours.
