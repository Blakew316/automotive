Warning signs

Your brakes usually tell you first.

Brakes wear gradually, so problems are easy to get used to. If you notice any of these, it’s time for an inspection.

Soft pedal or grinding? Don’t wait. Metal-on-metal contact and hydraulic problems can reduce stopping power quickly. Call us and we’ll advise whether it’s safe to drive in.

  • Squealing or squeaking when you brake, often the pad wear indicator
  • Grinding or growling, which can mean metal-to-metal contact
  • Pulsing or vibration in the pedal or steering wheel
  • A soft, spongy or sinking brake pedal
  • The vehicle pulls to one side when stopping
  • Brake or ABS warning light on the dash
  • A burning smell or smoke from a wheel after driving

Brake FAQ

Brake questions, answered.

Still unsure? Call us and describe what you’re noticing. We’ll tell you if it can wait.

Good to know: if you drive in stop-and-go traffic, tow or carry heavy loads, your brakes will wear faster than average. Ask us to check them at every oil change.

What are the signs my brakes need work?

Common signs include squealing or grinding, a pulsing or vibrating pedal, a soft or sinking pedal, the car pulling to one side, a brake or ABS warning light, or a burning smell after braking. If you notice any of these, have your brakes inspected soon.

Do I always need new rotors when I get new pads?

Not always. We measure rotor thickness against the manufacturer’s minimum and check for warping, scoring and heat spots. If a rotor is within spec and in good shape it may be reused. If not, we recommend resurfacing or replacement and show you the measurements.

How often should brake fluid be changed?

Many manufacturers recommend a brake fluid change every two to three years. Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time, which lowers its boiling point and can corrode internal parts. Your owner’s manual lists the interval for your vehicle.

Is it safe to drive with the brake warning light on?

A red brake light that stays on with the parking brake released can mean low fluid or a hydraulic problem, so have it checked right away. If the pedal feels soft, don’t drive it. Call us. An amber ABS light usually means normal braking still works but the anti-lock system may be disabled; have it checked soon.

How long does a brake job take?

It depends on the vehicle and what we find. Many pad and rotor jobs on one axle are finished the same day. We give you a time estimate along with your quote.
