When to book

The best time to inspect is before you need to.

An inspection usually costs far less than the surprise it helps you avoid. These are the moments when it pays off most.

Buying from a private seller? Private sales are usually as-is, so once you pay, the problems are yours. Get the inspection first, then negotiate or walk away with confidence.

  • You’re about to buy a used car, truck or SUV
  • There are no service records, or the price seems too good to be true
  • A long road trip, a move or a summer of towing is coming up
  • Winter or summer is on the way and it hasn’t been checked in a while
  • You just bought a car and don’t know much about its history
  • A new leak, noise or warning light showed up right before a big trip
  • Your mower, generator or snow blower is about to go back to work

Pre-purchase inspections

Buying used? Know before you pay.

A used car can drive fine around the block and still hide expensive problems. We check the things that matter most, then explain them in plain English.

What we check

  • Engine: how it starts, idles and runs
  • Transmission: shifting and fluid
  • Leaks: oil, coolant and other fluids
  • Frame & rust: underbody and signs of past damage
  • Brakes: pads, rotors, lines and fluid
  • Tires: tread depth, wear and age
  • Suspension: shocks, joints and steering
  • Codes: a scan for stored trouble codes
  • Test drive: how it tracks, shifts and stops

The seller can bring it in. Ask them to drop the car off, or bring it by yourself with their OK. We’ll go over the results with you, the buyer.

Inspection FAQ

Inspection questions, answered.

Have a car in mind? Call us with the year, make and model and we’ll talk you through what the inspection covers.

Good to know: an inspection is a snapshot of the vehicle on the day we see it. It can’t predict every future repair, but it takes most of the guesswork out of buying or traveling.

Why should I get a pre-purchase inspection?

A used car can look clean and drive fine around the block while hiding leaks, worn parts, rust or stored trouble codes. An independent inspection shows you what you’re buying, gives you facts to negotiate with and helps you walk away from a money pit.

How long does an inspection take?

It depends on the vehicle and the type of inspection. A road-trip or seasonal check is usually quicker than a full pre-purchase inspection, which includes a test drive and time on the lift. We’ll give you a time estimate when you book.

Do I need the seller’s permission to have a car inspected?

Yes. The seller has to agree to let the car come to our shop, and either they bring it in or you do with their OK. Most honest sellers welcome an independent inspection. If a seller refuses, that tells you something too.

What do I get when the inspection is done?

A written report that sorts what we found into good, watch and needs attention, plus a walk-through of the results in plain English. If something needs repair, we can give you an estimate so you know the real cost before you decide.

Can you inspect a car I’m selling?

Yes. A pre-sale inspection shows you what a buyer’s mechanic is likely to find. You can fix small issues, price the car fairly and share the report with buyers.
