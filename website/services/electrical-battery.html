Warning signs

Electrical trouble rarely picks a good time.

Batteries often fail with little warning, especially in extreme heat or cold. These are the early signs worth acting on.

Swollen, cracked or leaking battery? Don’t jump-start or charge it. A damaged battery can leak acid or release flammable gas. Call us and we’ll tell you the safest next step.

  • Slow, labored cranking, especially on cold mornings
  • Clicking when you turn the key or press start, but no crank
  • Headlights that dim at idle, flicker or brighten when you rev
  • A battery or charging warning light while you drive
  • White or blue-green crust around the battery terminals
  • A swollen battery case or a rotten-egg smell under the hood
  • Random glitches in windows, locks, the radio or gauges

Which one is it?

Battery, alternator or starter?

All three can leave you stuck, but each one tends to leave different clues. Here’s how they usually show up.

After sitting

Likely the battery

  • Slow, sluggish crank after the car sits overnight
  • Starts fine after a jump and keeps running
  • The battery is three to five years old or more
  • Corroded terminals or a swollen case
While driving

Likely the alternator

  • Battery or charging light comes on as you drive
  • Lights dim or flicker, or brighten when you rev
  • Dies again soon after a jump, or stalls on the road
  • Whining from the engine or a hot-wire smell
Turning the key

Likely the starter

  • One loud click, while the lights stay bright
  • Grinding or whirring without the engine turning
  • Starts only some of the time, after a few tries
  • Radio, lights and dash all work normally

Clues, not a verdict. A failing alternator can drain a good battery, and a weak battery can make a healthy starter sound tired. That’s why we test the whole system before recommending any parts.

Our electrical process

Test first, then fix.

  1. 1

    Test the battery

    Resting voltage, then a load or conductance test to see if it can still deliver real cranking power.

  2. 2

    Check charging & starting

    Alternator output with the engine running, starter draw while cranking, plus belt and cable condition.

  3. 3

    For drains, lighting or glitches, we follow the circuit with a meter and wiring diagrams to the actual cause.

    Repair & retest

    With your approval, we make the repair and retest the whole system before you get your keys.

Electrical FAQ

Battery & electrical questions, answered.

Car won’t start? Call and tell us what you hear when you turn the key. It narrows things down more than you’d think.

Good to know: summer heat does much of the damage to a battery, but the failure often shows up on the first cold morning. A quick test before winter can save you a no-start.

How long does a car battery last?

Often three to five years, though it varies with climate, driving habits and the battery itself. Heat is especially hard on batteries, and a steady diet of short trips can keep one from ever fully recharging. A quick test tells you whether yours is still healthy.

How can I tell if it’s the battery or the alternator?

Battery trouble usually shows up at startup: slow cranking or clicking, especially after the car has been sitting. Alternator trouble tends to show up while you drive: a battery warning light, dimming lights, or a car that dies again soon after a jump. Because one can mask the other, we test both.

Why does my battery keep dying?

Common causes include an aging battery, an alternator that isn’t charging properly, loose or corroded connections, lots of short trips, or a parasitic draw, meaning something keeps using power after the car is off. We check each of these instead of just swapping in another battery.

How do I jump-start my car safely?

Check your owner’s manual first, since some vehicles have dedicated jump points. In general: red clamp to the dead battery’s positive terminal, the other red clamp to the good battery’s positive, black to the good battery’s negative, and the last black clamp to bare, unpainted metal on the stalled car, away from the battery. Start the working car, then yours, and remove the cables in reverse order. Never jump a battery that is cracked, swollen, leaking or frozen.

How does battery testing work?

We start with resting voltage: a fully charged battery reads about 12.6 volts. Then a load or conductance tester measures how well it can deliver cranking power, which voltage alone can’t tell you. We also check charging voltage with the engine running, typically around 13.5 to 14.7 volts, to make sure the alternator is doing its job.
